Soil pH describes how acidic or alkaline the soil solution is. It controls how
available nutrients are to the crop, and it changes slowly.

General interpretation bands used for field screening:
- Below 5.5: strongly acidic. Phosphorus, calcium and magnesium availability drops,
  and aluminium or manganese can reach levels that damage roots.
- 5.5 to 6.5: slightly acidic. Suitable for most crops; good micronutrient availability.
- 6.5 to 7.5: near neutral. The widest nutrient availability band and the target for
  most field crops.
- 7.5 to 8.5: alkaline. Iron, zinc, manganese and phosphorus become less available.
  Zinc and iron deficiency symptoms are common in this band.
- Above 8.5: strongly alkaline, often with sodium problems. Soil structure and
  infiltration suffer.

What to do about it:
- Acidic soils are corrected with agricultural lime. The quantity depends on soil
  texture, buffering capacity and the measured pH; it must come from a laboratory
  lime requirement test, not from a field pH reading alone.
- Alkaline and sodic soils are managed with gypsum, organic matter, and improved
  drainage. Gypsum rate also requires a laboratory sodium or gypsum requirement test.
- Organic matter (farmyard manure, compost, green manure) buffers pH in both
  directions and improves nutrient holding capacity.

Important caveat: a handheld or in-soil probe reading is a screening value. It is
affected by soil moisture, probe contact, temperature and calibration drift. Before
spending money on lime or gypsum, confirm with a laboratory soil test.
