Nutrient recommendations should come from a soil test, the crop, the yield target and
the nutrients already applied. A sensor NPK reading is a screening indicator, not a
laboratory soil test, and must not be used alone to prescribe fertiliser.

Nutrient content of common fertilisers (per 100 kg of product):
- Urea: 46 kg nitrogen.
- DAP (di-ammonium phosphate): 18 kg nitrogen and 46 kg P2O5.
- SSP (single super phosphate): 16 kg P2O5 plus sulphur and calcium.
- MOP (muriate of potash): 60 kg K2O.
- NPK 12:32:16 complex: 12 kg N, 32 kg P2O5, 16 kg K2O.

How to convert a recommendation into product quantity:
1. Start with the recommended N, P2O5 and K2O per hectare.
2. Satisfy the phosphorus requirement first, usually with DAP or SSP.
3. Subtract the nitrogen supplied by DAP from the nitrogen requirement.
4. Supply the remaining nitrogen with urea, split across basal and top dressings.
5. Supply potassium with MOP.

Timing principles:
- Phosphorus and potassium are usually applied as a basal dose at or before sowing,
  placed near the seed row rather than broadcast.
- Nitrogen is split. A common pattern is one third at sowing and the remainder in two
  top dressings timed to crop stage and irrigation.
- Top dress nitrogen when soil moisture is adequate, not on a dry surface, and not
  immediately before heavy rain.

Organic sources: farmyard manure, compost and green manure supply nutrients slowly,
improve soil structure and raise water holding capacity. They complement, rather than
replace, a targeted mineral dose in an intensive system.

Never calculate a dose from an unverified sensor value, an assumed yield target or an
unknown product composition. If any of those three is missing, say so and ask.
