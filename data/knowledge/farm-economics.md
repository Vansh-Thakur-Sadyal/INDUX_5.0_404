A cost and return comparison is only as good as its assumptions, so every assumption
must be stated alongside the number.

Cost components for one crop on one parcel:
- Seed, and seed treatment.
- Fertiliser and manure, priced at what the farmer actually pays.
- Crop protection products.
- Irrigation: diesel or electricity, and labour for application.
- Machinery: tillage, sowing, harvesting and threshing, whether owned or hired.
- Labour: family and hired, counted separately so the farmer can see both.
- Post-harvest: bagging, transport to market, market fee and commission.
- Land rent, where the land is leased.

Revenue:
- Expected yield multiplied by expected price, plus by-product value such as straw or
  fodder, which can be a significant share in cereals.

How to compare two options honestly:
- Hold everything constant except the choice being compared.
- Show the break-even yield and the break-even price, because those two numbers tell the
  farmer how much room for error exists.
- Present a range, not a single number, when yield or price is uncertain.

An estimated profit is not a guarantee. Yield depends on weather and management, and
price depends on a market nobody controls.
