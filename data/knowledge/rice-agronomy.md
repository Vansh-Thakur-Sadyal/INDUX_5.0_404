Rice is the principal kharif cereal and the largest agricultural water user in India.

Establishment methods:
- Transplanted puddled rice: nursery raised for 21 to 30 days, then transplanted into
  puddled, levelled fields.
- Direct seeded rice (DSR): seed drilled into a prepared field, saving water and labour
  but demanding stricter weed management.

Water management:
- Continuous flooding is not required for yield. Alternate wetting and drying (AWD),
  where the field is re-irrigated when the water level falls to about 15 cm below the
  surface in a field tube, can cut water use substantially without yield loss.
- Keep standing water during the critical panicle initiation to flowering window.
- Drain the field about 10 days before harvest to aid machine operation and grain dry down.

Stages: nursery, transplanting shock and recovery, active tillering, panicle
initiation, booting, heading and flowering, milk, dough, maturity.

Nutrient notes: rice responds strongly to nitrogen, which must be split because
flooded soils lose nitrogen through denitrification and volatilisation. Zinc
deficiency is common in puddled alkaline soils and shows as bronze patches on older
leaves several weeks after transplanting.

Residue: burning rice straw destroys organic carbon and nutrients and pollutes the
air. Incorporation, mulching with a happy seeder style operation, baling for fodder or
industry, and composting are the usable alternatives.
