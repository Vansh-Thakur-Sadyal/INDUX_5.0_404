Electrical conductivity (EC) measures the salt content of the soil solution. It is
reported in deciSiemens per metre (dS/m). The exact value depends on the measurement
method, so a sensor reading and a laboratory saturation-extract reading are not
directly comparable.

General screening bands for a 1:2 soil-water extract or an in-soil sensor:
- Below 0.8 dS/m: low salinity. No salt-related yield effect expected.
- 0.8 to 1.6 dS/m: normal for a fertilised field. Watch sensitive crops.
- 1.6 to 3.0 dS/m: moderate. Salt sensitive crops such as beans, onion and many
  vegetables begin to lose yield.
- Above 3.0 dS/m: high. Germination and early growth are affected in most crops.
  Salt tolerant crops such as barley, cotton and some millets cope better.

Causes and management:
- Irrigation with saline groundwater is the most common cause in India.
- Poor drainage concentrates salts in the root zone, especially in summer.
- Leaching with good quality water, where drainage allows, lowers root-zone salts.
- Gypsum helps when the problem is sodium (sodicity), not just total salts.
- Raised beds, mulching and placing seed away from the ridge centre reduce salt
  injury at germination.

A high EC reading immediately after fertiliser application is expected and does not
mean the field is saline. Re-read after an irrigation cycle before concluding.
