Weather changes which operations are safe and useful on a given day.

Spraying:
- Avoid spraying when wind exceeds roughly 10 to 15 km per hour; drift wastes product
  and harms neighbouring fields.
- Avoid the hottest part of the day in summer; evaporation and volatilisation cut
  efficacy and raise operator risk.
- Rain within a few hours of application washes most products off. Check the rain
  forecast and the product label rainfastness note.

Irrigation:
- Do not irrigate ahead of significant forecast rain.
- Waterlogging after heavy rain damages roots; open drainage channels where possible.

Temperature:
- Terminal heat during grain filling in wheat shrivels grain. A light irrigation can
  moderate canopy temperature during a heat spell.
- Frost risk in north India in December and January damages potato, mustard flowering
  and vegetables; light irrigation on the evening before a frost night reduces damage.

Humidity: prolonged high humidity with moderate temperature favours fungal disease.
Scout more frequently after such spells rather than spraying preventively by default.

Data freshness matters. A forecast more than a few hours old can mislead. If the
weather source cannot be reached, say that the forecast is unavailable instead of
answering from an old value.
