Crop residue is organic matter and nutrients. Burning destroys both and pollutes the
air; it is also prohibited in several states.

Usable alternatives:
- In-situ incorporation: chop and mix residue into the soil, ideally with adequate
  moisture and some nitrogen to support decomposition, since high carbon to nitrogen
  residue temporarily immobilises soil nitrogen.
- Mulching: leave residue on the surface and sow the next crop through it with a
  suitable machine. This conserves moisture, moderates soil temperature and suppresses
  weeds.
- Baling: remove bales for fodder, bedding, mushroom cultivation, board or biomass
  industry. This brings income but exports nutrients from the field.
- Composting and vermicomposting: slower, but produces a high quality amendment.
- Biochar and biogas feedstock, where local facilities exist.

Choosing between them depends on the time available before the next sowing, the
machinery that can be hired locally, the labour cost, and whether the residue has a
local market.

What not to claim: specific carbon sequestration or emission reduction figures require
measurement. Describe the direction of the effect and the mechanism, not an invented
quantity.
