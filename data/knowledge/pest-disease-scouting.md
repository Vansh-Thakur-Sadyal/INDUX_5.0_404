A photograph or a verbal symptom description narrows the possibilities; it rarely
proves a cause. Nutrient deficiency, water stress, herbicide damage, insect feeding and
disease can all produce yellowing or spots.

Scouting method:
- Walk a W or zig-zag pattern across the field; inspect at least five spots.
- Look at the underside of leaves, the growing point, the stem base and the roots of a
  few plants.
- Note how damage is distributed: patchy and spreading from field edges suggests pests
  moving in; uniform across the field suggests nutrition, water or soil.
- Record the date, the stage and the proportion of plants affected.

Integrated pest management, in order of preference:
1. Prevention: resistant varieties, clean seed, crop rotation, sowing time, field
   sanitation, removal of crop residue harbouring the pest.
2. Cultural and physical: trap crops, pheromone and light traps, hand removal of
   affected leaves, balanced nitrogen (excess nitrogen attracts sucking pests).
3. Biological: conserve natural enemies such as ladybird beetles, spiders and parasitic
   wasps; avoid broad spectrum sprays that kill them.
4. Chemical: only after the problem is identified and the damage justifies it.

Important: product choice, dose, dilution, spray interval, pre-harvest interval and
protective equipment must come from the product label approved for that crop and pest
in India, or from a local agriculture officer. Never act on a dose quoted from memory,
from an unverified source, or from a chat assistant.
