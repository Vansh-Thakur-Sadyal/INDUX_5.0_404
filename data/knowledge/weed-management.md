Weeds compete for water, nutrients, light and space. The critical period of crop-weed
competition is usually the first third of the crop cycle; weeds removed after that
period cost much less yield, though they still add seed to the soil bank.

Management options:
- Cultural: timely sowing, correct seed rate and row spacing for quick canopy closure,
  crop rotation, clean seed, and clean field bunds and channels.
- Mechanical: a pre-sowing stale seedbed, hand weeding, wheel hoe or power weeder
  between rows. Two mechanical operations at the right time often substitute for a
  herbicide.
- Mulching: crop residue or plastic mulch suppresses germination and conserves moisture.
- Biological: limited practical options in field crops in India today.
- Chemical: pre-emergence and post-emergence herbicides exist for most crops, but the
  choice depends on the weed species present, the crop, the crop stage and the following
  crop in the rotation. Residual herbicides can damage the next crop.

Herbicide resistance is a growing problem, notably in Phalaris minor in wheat. Repeating
the same molecule year after year selects for resistant populations. Rotating modes of
action and mixing non-chemical methods delays resistance.

As with insecticides, the specific product, rate and timing must come from the approved
label or a local agriculture officer, never from an unverified source.
