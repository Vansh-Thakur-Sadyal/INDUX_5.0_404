Irrigation scheduling answers two questions: when to irrigate and how much.

When to irrigate:
- Soil water is held between field capacity (soil full after drainage) and permanent
  wilting point (plant cannot extract more). The difference is available water.
- Most field crops should be irrigated before 50 percent of available water is used.
  Sensitive crops and shallow-rooted vegetables need irrigation earlier, around 25 to
  35 percent depletion.
- A volumetric moisture sensor reading alone does not give depletion percentage. It
  must be compared against the field capacity and wilting point of that soil texture.

Indicative volumetric water content by texture (percent by volume):
- Sandy soil: field capacity around 10 to 15, wilting point around 4 to 6.
- Loam: field capacity around 25 to 30, wilting point around 10 to 14.
- Clay loam and clay: field capacity around 35 to 45, wilting point around 18 to 24.

How much to apply:
- Depth of water to replace equals root-zone depth multiplied by the depletion in
  volumetric terms, divided by the irrigation application efficiency.
- Typical application efficiency: flood or border 50 to 60 percent, furrow 60 to 70
  percent, sprinkler 70 to 80 percent, drip 85 to 95 percent.

Practical checks before irrigating:
- Check the forecast. Irrigating before significant rain wastes water and can cause
  waterlogging.
- A single point sensor may not represent the whole field. Sandy patches, field slope
  and the position of the sensor relative to an emitter change the reading.
- Critical irrigation stages differ by crop; missing them costs more yield than a
  general shortage at a non-critical stage.
