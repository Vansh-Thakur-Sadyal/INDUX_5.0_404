Agmarknet publishes daily arrivals and prices for commodities across regulated markets
(mandis) in India. Each record carries a market, a commodity, a variety, a date, and
minimum, maximum and modal prices, normally per quintal.

How to read it:
- The modal price is the most frequently traded price and is the most useful single
  number. The minimum and maximum show the spread between quality grades on that day.
- Compare like with like: the same commodity and the same variety or grade. A different
  variety explains most surprising gaps between markets.
- Check the date on every record. Prices from a week ago are not today's prices, and
  many markets do not report every day.
- A higher price in a distant market is not automatically better. Subtract transport,
  labour, market fee, commission and the risk of a price change in transit.

What cannot be claimed:
- Future prices cannot be predicted. A trend over recent weeks is a description of what
  happened, not a forecast.
- Without enough history for that market and commodity, no trend should be stated at all.

If live price data is unavailable, the honest answer is that it is unavailable, with the
date of the most recent figure that is actually held.
