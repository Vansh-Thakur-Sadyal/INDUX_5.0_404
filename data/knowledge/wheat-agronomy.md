Wheat is the main rabi cereal across north and central India. It is sown after the
kharif harvest and matures in roughly 110 to 145 days depending on variety and zone.

Sowing:
- Timely sowing in the north-western plains is generally early November; sowing much
  later shortens the grain filling window and exposes the crop to terminal heat.
- A seed rate of about 100 kg per hectare is common for timely sown bold-seeded
  varieties, raised for late sowing.
- Row spacing of about 20 to 22.5 cm; sowing depth of 5 to 6 cm into moist soil.

Growth stages and what they need:
- Crown root initiation (about 20 to 25 days after sowing): the single most critical
  irrigation. Missing it costs the most yield of any stage.
- Tillering (about 40 to 45 days): second irrigation and usually a nitrogen top dress.
- Jointing and booting (about 60 to 70 days): rapid biomass growth, high water demand.
- Flowering and anthesis (about 85 to 95 days): heat and moisture stress here directly
  reduces grain number.
- Milk and dough stage (about 100 to 115 days): grain filling; late water stress
  shrivels grain.
- Physiological maturity and harvest: grain moisture falls; harvest when the crop is
  dry enough for safe storage.

Common problems: yellow rust in cool humid weather, aphids in late-sown crops, and
lodging in over-fertilised irrigated fields. Confirm any suspected disease with a
local agriculture officer before applying a product.
