Most management decisions depend on the crop stage, not the calendar date.

How the stage is established, in order of reliability:
1. Farmer observation of the plant (tillers visible, flowering started, grain in milk).
2. Days after sowing, when the sowing date is known and the season is normal.
3. Growing degree days, when daily temperature data is available.

If the sowing date is unknown and the farmer cannot describe the plant, the stage is
unknown. Ask one simple question instead of assuming: has the crop started to flower,
are there ears or pods, and how tall is it.

Why the stage drives everything:
- Irrigation: critical stages differ by crop. Water at a critical stage is worth far
  more than the same water at a non-critical stage.
- Nitrogen: top dressing is effective during vegetative growth and tillering, wasteful
  after flowering.
- Crop protection: pre-harvest intervals apply from the application date to harvest, so
  closeness to maturity restricts which products can be used at all.
- Market planning: a reliable harvest window lets the farmer plan storage and sale.
