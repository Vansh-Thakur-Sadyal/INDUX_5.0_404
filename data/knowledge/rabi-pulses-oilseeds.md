Mustard (sarson, rai):
- Rabi oilseed, roughly 110 to 140 days, grown on light to medium soils.
- Sown October to early November; needs relatively little irrigation, often two to
  three applications, with the pre-flowering irrigation most important.
- Tolerates mildly saline and alkaline soils better than wheat, which makes it a
  useful option where EC or pH is marginal.
- Aphids in warm humid spells during flowering are the main pest concern.

Chickpea (chana, gram):
- Rabi pulse, roughly 95 to 130 days, grown largely rainfed on residual moisture.
- Fixes atmospheric nitrogen, so nitrogen requirement is low; a small starter dose only.
- Excess irrigation or heavy nitrogen causes vegetative growth at the cost of pods.
- Wilt and pod borer are the recurring problems; crop rotation reduces wilt pressure.

Lentil and barley: both suit low input, light irrigation situations. Barley is one of
the more salt tolerant cereals and is a reasonable choice on saline patches.

Why rotation matters: a cereal followed by a legume restores nitrogen, breaks pest and
disease cycles, and spreads market risk across crops with different price behaviour.
